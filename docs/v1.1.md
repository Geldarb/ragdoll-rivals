# 1.1 — active balance, gait and smoother motion

Scope approved by Bradley: active upright balance/recovery, proper planted legs, buffered smooth networking. No camera zoom, damage, stamina, style cadence, weapon timings or match timeout changes.

## Physics

Added a connected pelvis and two physical feet (14 bodies per fighter). Torso, head, pelvis and leg muscles use bounded inertia-scaled proportional/derivative torque. Legs have stiffer .98 pin constraints, soft hip/knee angle limits, inverse-geometry target angles and alternating foot swing/stance. A stance foot can plant against the ground; a swing foot lifts using forces, never position teleportation. Finite vertical posture-support forces let collapsed fighters stand back up; they switch off during stagger. Invisible side collision boundaries prevent stepping off the original ground slab; viewport/camera dimensions are unchanged.

Unblocked impacts of at least 18 damage disable balance and gait for `1.45 - balance*.065` seconds, followed by a balance-dependent muscle ramp. Heavy hits still produce angular and linear impulse. Recovery is physical; an opponent can interrupt it. High balance makes recovery quicker, not invulnerable. Arm AI and gear tuning are unchanged.

A collision stress test found excessive damping feedback from overlapping knee/angle-limit muscles. Lower damping plus a shared finite torque cap fixes the source rather than clamping or teleporting body positions. Twelve seeded collision matches now remain finite and inside the vertical arena bounds.

## Networking and rendering

Simulation remains 30 Hz with two 60 Hz Matter substeps. Snapshots are now 30 Hz, rounded to 0.1 pixel / .001 radian. Static fighter/build metadata is sent once in `started`. A 12-frame client buffer renders 100 ms behind server time using shortest-path angular interpolation. Missing packets hold the last pose; no unbounded extrapolation. Rematches clear timing and interpolation state.

A wall-clock accumulator wakes every 8 ms, performs fixed simulation steps and catches up to five steps after a late wake. It drops only excess debt beyond five steps to avoid an overload spiral, reporting all dropped milliseconds instead of silently slowing down. Once-per-second snapshot telemetry reports measured simulation Hz, mean/max physics step time and dropped time. Rendering remains requestAnimationFrame at device rate, avoids hidden-arena drawing and updates existing HUD fills rather than rebuilding its DOM on each packet. Install updates activate a waiting service worker on the user's update button.

## Evidence and limitations

Baseline live Chromium phone-emulated sample: 59.63 rendering FPS; 15.035 snapshots/s; 30.070 simulation ticks/s; 2630 mean snapshot bytes; torso at floor y=434.95 and angle 1.271 radians. This does **not** measure Bradley's actual Samsung browser or establish that his physical phone draws at 60 FPS. Baseline server step-duration telemetry did not exist.

Stable local sample: 60.00 FPS; 30.019 snapshots/s; 30.019 simulation ticks/s; 1472 mean snapshot bytes. Server mean step .337 ms, maximum 6.581 ms, zero dropped milliseconds over eight seconds. See saved JSON for final live measurements.

Automated requirements: ten-second unprovoked walking >85% upright, genuine heavy-impact topple and <6-second recovery, balance-dependent recovery, alternating planted/lifted feet, seeded collision stability, 100 ms interpolation/angle wrap, bounded buffer, catch-up/debt handling, compact payload and real snapshot throughput. Existing tests retain their behaviours; connected-body count changes from 11 to 14 because of pelvis/feet, and health version changes to 1.1.0.

Remaining limitations: gait is deliberately simple, not human animation; foot slips and body overlap can occur under impacts. Network stalls longer than the buffer hold poses. Free Render is not guaranteed to sustain many simultaneous fights; reported telemetry covers the measured load only. No physical Samsung device is attached for direct FPS verification.
